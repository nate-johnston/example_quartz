The Psalm of the Cross  
([Matthew 27:32–56](https://biblehub.com/matthew/27.htm#32); [Mark 15:21–41](https://biblehub.com/mark/15.htm#21); [Luke 23:26–43](https://biblehub.com/luke/23.htm#26); [John 19:16–30](https://biblehub.com/john/19.htm#16))

For the choirmaster. To the tune of “The Doe of the Dawn.” A Psalm of David.

[**1**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#) My God, my God,

why have You forsaken me?[a](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn)

Why are You so far from saving me,

so far from my words of groaning?

[**2**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-2.htm) I cry out by day, O my God,

but You do not answer,

and by night,

but I have no rest.

[**3**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-3.htm) Yet You are holy,

enthroned on the praises of Israel.

[**4**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-4.htm) In You our fathers trusted;

they trusted and You delivered them.

[**5**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-5.htm) They cried out to You and were set free;

they trusted in You and were not disappointed.

[**6**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-6.htm) But I am a worm and not a man,

scorned by men and despised by the people.

[**7**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-7.htm) All who see me mock me;

they sneer and shake their heads:

[**8**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-8.htm) “He trusts in the LORD,

let the LORD deliver him;

let the LORD rescue him,

since He delights in him.” [b](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn)

[**9**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-9.htm) Yet You brought me forth from the womb;

You made me secure at my mother’s breast.

[**10**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-10.htm) From birth I was cast upon You;

from my mother’s womb You have been my God.

[**11**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-11.htm) Be not far from me,

for trouble is near

and there is no one to help.

[**12**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-12.htm) Many bulls surround me;

strong bulls of Bashan encircle me.

[**13**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-13.htm) They open their jaws against me

like lions that roar and maul.

[**14**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-14.htm) I am poured out like water,

and all my bones are disjointed.

My heart is like wax;

it melts away within me.

[**15**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-15.htm) My strength [c](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn) is dried up like a potsherd,

and my tongue sticks to the roof of my mouth.

You lay me in the dust of death.

[**16**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-16.htm) For dogs surround me;

a band of evil men encircles me;

they have pierced my hands and feet.[d](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn)

[**17**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-17.htm) I can count all my bones;

they stare and gloat over me.

[**18**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-18.htm) They divide my garments among them

and cast lots for my clothing.[e](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn)

[**19**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-19.htm) But You, O LORD, be not far off;

O my Strength, come quickly to help me.

[**20**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-20.htm) Deliver my soul from the sword,

my precious life from the power of wild dogs.

[**21**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-21.htm) Save me from the mouth of the lion;

at the horns of the wild oxen You have answered me!

[**22**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-22.htm) I will proclaim Your name to my brothers;

I will praise You in the assembly.[f](https://biblehub.com/psalms/#fn)

[**23**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-23.htm) You who fear the LORD, praise Him!

All descendants of Jacob, honor Him!

All offspring of Israel, revere Him!

[**24**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-24.htm) For He has not despised or detested

the torment of the afflicted.

He has not hidden His face from him,

but has attended to his cry for help.

[**25**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-25.htm) My praise for You resounds in the great assembly;

I will fulfill my vows before those who fear You.

[**26**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-26.htm) The poor will eat and be satisfied;

those who seek the LORD will praise Him.

May your hearts live forever!

[**27**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-27.htm) All the ends of the earth

will remember and turn to the LORD.

All the families of the nations

will bow down before Him.

[**28**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-28.htm) For dominion belongs to the LORD

and He rules over the nations.

[**29**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-29.htm) All the rich of the earth will feast and worship;

all who go down to the dust will kneel before Him—

even those unable to preserve their lives.

[**30**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-30.htm) Posterity will serve Him;

they will declare the Lord to a new generation.

[**31**](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-31.htm) They will come and proclaim His righteousness

to a people yet unborn—

all that He has done.

**The Psalm of the Cross**

**Verses 1–2 – Cry of Abandonment**  
David begins with the haunting words, “My God, My God, why have You forsaken me?” He feels unheard and far from rescue though he prays day and night.

**Verses 3–5 – Appeal to God’s Faithfulness**  
Even while suffering, David recalls God’s holiness and past deliverances for Israel’s ancestors, reminding himself that trust in the LORD has never been in vain.

**Verses 6–8 – Scorn from Onlookers**  
The psalmist is mocked as a “worm and not a man.” Observers sneer, “He trusted in the LORD, let Him deliver him,” amplifying his agony.

**Verses 9–11 – Lifelong Dependence on God**  
David reflects on God’s care since birth, pleading, “Do not be far from me, for trouble is near and there is no one to help.”

**Verses 12–18 – Physical and Emotional Collapse**  
Surrounded by fierce enemies (“strong bulls of Bashan”), his strength drains, bones are out of joint, and lots are cast for his garments—graphic pictures later echoed at the cross.

**Verses 19–21 – Urgent Plea for Rescue**  
Turning point: “But You, O LORD, be not far off.” David asks God to save him from the sword, the lion’s mouth, and the horns of wild oxen.

**Verses 22–24 – Public Praise Promised**  
Confident of deliverance, he vows to declare God’s name to the congregation, urging others, “You who fear the LORD, praise Him!”

**Verses 25–26 – Vows Fulfilled & The Poor Satisfied**  
Deliverance will spark thanksgiving offerings, and “the meek will eat and be satisfied,” showing God’s care for the needy.

**Verses 27–31 – Worldwide Worship & Future Hope**  
The psalm soars: all nations will remember the LORD; future generations will proclaim, “He has done it!”—a glimpse of global, lasting praise.

---

Psalm 22 is a poignant composition by David that conveys the deep distress of someone who, despite feeling forsaken by God, continues to express steadfast faith. The Psalm begins with a heart-wrenching cry for help, takes the reader through an intense description of suffering, and concludes with a confident prophecy of deliverance and global worship.

**Authorship and Setting**

[Psalm 22](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/22.htm) carries the superscription, “For the choirmaster. To the tune of ‘The Doe of the Dawn.’ A Psalm of David.” Written by Israel’s shepherd-king, the song reflects real personal agony, yet its language stretches far beyond any event we know from David’s life. He likely composed it during a season of intense persecution—perhaps during Saul’s relentless pursuit ([1 Samuel 23–24](https://biblehub.com/bsb/1_samuel/23.htm)) or Absalom’s rebellion ([2 Samuel 15](https://biblehub.com/bsb/2_samuel/15.htm)).

**Structure and Flow**

1\. Lament (vv. 1–21a)

2\. Pivot (v. 21b)

3\. Praise (vv. 22–31)

The psalm arcs from darkness to light, from forsakenness to worldwide worship. That turn in the middle—“You have answered me” (v. 21b, literal Hebrew)—is the hinge.

**Prophetic Foreshadowing of the Crucifixion**

• Jesus quoted verse 1 on the cross ([Matthew 27:46](https://biblehub.com/matthew/27-46.htm); [Mark 15:34](https://biblehub.com/mark/15-34.htm)).

• Verse 7–8 matches the mockery at the crucifixion ([Matthew 27:39-43](https://biblehub.com/matthew/27-39.htm)).

• Verse 16, “They pierced my hands and feet,” mirrors nail wounds ([John 20:25-27](https://biblehub.com/john/20-25.htm)).

• Verse 18 fulfilled when soldiers cast lots for His clothing ([John 19:23-24](https://biblehub.com/john/19-23.htm)).

The psalm offers a first-person window into Messiah’s sufferings a millennium before Jesus was born.

**New Testament Echoes**

• [Hebrews 2:12](https://biblehub.com/hebrews/2-12.htm) cites verse 22 to show Jesus leading believers in praise.

• [Revelation 5](https://biblehub.com/bsb/revelation/5.htm) & 7 echo the global worship scene of verses 27-31.

• [Romans 15:9](https://biblehub.com/romans/15-9.htm) reflects the call for Gentiles to glorify God, hinted here.

**Historical and Archaeological Notes**

• Crucifixion Evidence: First-century ossuaries (e.g., Yehohanan’s heel bone with a nail) confirm the practice exactly as [Psalm 22](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/22.htm) depicts.

• Garment Casting: Dice and knucklebones found at Roman sites illustrate the soldiers’ lot-casting in verse 18.

• “Bulls of Bashan”: Bashan’s basalt plateau east of the Jordan was famed for its strong cattle ([Amos 4:1](https://biblehub.com/amos/4-1.htm)). The image signals powerful, intimidating foes.

• “Piercing” of hands and feet matches Dead Sea Scroll fragments (Nahal Hever) that favor the reading “pierced” over “like a lion,” supporting the messianic view.

**God’s Character on Display**

• Holiness (v. 3)

• Faithfulness across generations (vv. 4-5)

• Nearness to the afflicted (v. 24)

• Global King (vv. 27-28)

Though David feels abandoned, the psalm ultimately affirms God’s unbreakable commitment to His people.

**Practical Takeaways**

• Honest Lament: Believers can pour out raw emotion without fear; God receives it.

• Remember Past Deliverances: Rehearsing God’s track record fuels hope when present rescue seems absent.

• Suffering Precedes Glory: Just as the psalm turns to praise, trials in Christ lead to eventual victory.

• Gospel Mission: The closing verses propel us toward global witness, confident that “all the families of the nations will bow down.”

**Links to Other Scriptures**

• [Job 30:17-19](https://biblehub.com/job/30-17.htm) shares similar bodily anguish.

• [Isaiah 53](https://biblehub.com/bsb/isaiah/53.htm) amplifies the suffering servant theme.

• [Philippians 2:8-11](https://biblehub.com/philippians/2-8.htm) parallels the path from self-emptying death to universal worship.

• [Psalm 16](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/16.htm) and [Psalm 110](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/110.htm) join [Psalm 22](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/22.htm) as a trio of key messianic psalms quoted in the New Testament.

**The Song’s Journey Through History**

• Early Church: [Psalm 22](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/22.htm) was read during Passion Week liturgies.

• Reformation: Martin Luther called it a “gem among psalms” for its clear witness to Christ.

• Modern Worship: Lines like “You answered me” inspire songs that blend lament and praise today.

**Conclusion**

[Psalm 22](https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/22.htm) begins in the darkest night of the soul and ends with a sunrise of worldwide praise. It assures believers that God hears every cry, remembers every promise, and in Christ has already secured the final shout: “He has done it!”

Teaching Points

**[The Cry of Despair](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_cry_of_despair.htm)**  
The psalm opens with a profound sense of abandonment, reflecting both David's personal struggles and the future suffering of Christ ([Psalm 22:1-2](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22.htm)).  
  
**[Trust in God's Faithfulness](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/trust_in_god's_faithfulness.htm)**  
Despite his anguish, David recalls God's past faithfulness to Israel, highlighting the importance of remembering God's deliverance ([Psalm 22:3-5](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-3.htm)).  
  
**[The Suffering Servant](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_suffering_servant.htm)**  
The vivid imagery of suffering in verses 6-18 parallels the crucifixion of Jesus, emphasizing the prophetic nature of this psalm ([Psalm 22:6-18](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-6.htm)).  
  
**[A Shift to Praise](https://biblehub.com/topical/a/a_shift_to_praise.htm)**  
The tone shifts from lament to praise as David anticipates God's deliverance and vows to declare His name to future generations ([Psalm 22:22-31](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-22.htm)).  
  
**[Messianic Fulfillment](https://biblehub.com/topical/m/messianic_fulfillment.htm)**  
The psalm is a powerful testament to the messianic prophecy, fulfilled in the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

People

1\. **[The Psalmist (David)](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_psalmist.htm)**  
The primary speaker in the psalm, traditionally identified as King David. He expresses deep anguish and a sense of abandonment by God, yet ultimately trusts in God's deliverance. The psalm begins with the cry, "My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" ([Psalm 22:1](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22.htm)), which is echoed by Jesus on the cross in the New Testament.  
  
2\. **[God](https://biblehub.com/topical/g/god.htm)**  
Referred to throughout the psalm as the one whom the psalmist calls upon for help and deliverance. The psalmist acknowledges God's holiness and past faithfulness to Israel, saying, "Yet You are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel" ([Psalm 22:3](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-3.htm)).  
  
3\. **[The Ancestors (Fathers of Israel)](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_ancestors.htm)**  
Mentioned as those who trusted in God and were delivered. The psalmist recalls, "In You our fathers trusted; they trusted and You delivered them" ([Psalm 22:4](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-4.htm)).  
  
4\. **[The Mockers (People)](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_mockers.htm)**  
Those who ridicule and scorn the psalmist, reflecting the mockery Jesus faced during His crucifixion. The psalmist describes them: "All who see me mock me; they sneer and shake their heads" ([Psalm 22:7](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-7.htm)).  
  
5\. **[The Congregation of the Righteous](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_congregation_of_the_righteous.htm)**  
Implied as those who will ultimately praise God for His deliverance. The psalmist anticipates declaring God's name to his brothers and praising Him in the assembly: "I will proclaim Your name to my brothers; I will praise You in the assembly" ([Psalm 22:22](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-22.htm)).  
  
6\. **[The Afflicted (Poor)](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_afflicted.htm)**  
Those who will be satisfied and praise God, as mentioned towards the end of the psalm. The psalmist declares, "The poor will eat and be satisfied; those who seek the LORD will praise Him" ([Psalm 22:26](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-26.htm)).  
  
7\. **[Future Generations](https://biblehub.com/topical/f/future_generations.htm)**  
Implied as those who will hear of God's righteousness and acts of deliverance. The psalm concludes with a vision of future generations serving the Lord: "They will proclaim His righteousness to a people yet unborn—for He has done it" ([Psalm 22:31](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-31.htm)).  
  
In the original Hebrew, the psalm uses terms like "אֵלִי" (Eli, "My God") and "אָבוֹתֵינוּ" (avotenu, "our fathers"), which emphasize the personal and communal relationship with God. The psalm's vivid imagery and prophetic elements have made it a central text in Christian theology, particularly in its connection to the passion of Christ.

Places

1\. **[The Assembly of the Wicked](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_assembly_of_the_wicked.htm)**  
This is a metaphorical place mentioned in [Psalm 22:16](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-16.htm), "For dogs surround me; a band of evil men encircles me." The "assembly" or "band" refers to a gathering of those who oppose and persecute the psalmist, symbolizing a place of hostility and danger.  
  
2\. **[The Dust of Death](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_dust_of_death.htm)**  
In [Psalm 22:15](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-15.htm), the psalmist says, "You lay me in the dust of death." This phrase symbolizes a place of extreme suffering and near-death, reflecting the psalmist's deep distress and sense of abandonment.  
  
3\. **[The Horns of the Wild Oxen](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_horns_of_the_wild_oxen.htm)**  
In [Psalm 22:21](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-21.htm), the psalmist cries out for deliverance "from the horns of the wild oxen." This imagery represents a place of peril and threat, as wild oxen were known for their strength and danger in ancient times.  
  
4\. **[The Congregation](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_congregation.htm)**  
[Psalm 22:22](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-22.htm) states, "I will proclaim Your name to my brothers; I will praise You in the assembly." Here, the "congregation" or "assembly" is a place of worship and community, where the psalmist vows to praise God among fellow believers.  
  
5\. **[The Ends of the Earth](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_ends_of_the_earth.htm)**  
In [Psalm 22:27](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-27.htm), it is written, "All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the LORD." This phrase signifies a place of universal reach, indicating that the message of God's deliverance will extend to all corners of the world.  
  
6\. **[The Great Assembly](https://biblehub.com/topical/t/the_great_assembly.htm)**  
[Psalm 22:25](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-25.htm) mentions, "From You comes my praise in the great assembly." This "great assembly" is a place of public worship and testimony, where the psalmist acknowledges God's faithfulness before a large gathering.

Events

1\. **[Cry of Abandonment](https://biblehub.com/topical/c/cry_of_abandonment.htm)**  
The psalm begins with a profound cry of abandonment, "My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" ([Psalm 22:1](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22.htm)). This expression of distress is echoed in the New Testament by Jesus on the cross, highlighting a deep sense of separation from God.  
  
2\. **[Plea for Deliverance](https://biblehub.com/topical/p/plea_for_deliverance.htm)**  
The psalmist pleads for God's help, expressing trust in God's past faithfulness to Israel, "In You our fathers trusted; they trusted and You delivered them" ([Psalm 22:4](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-4.htm)). This reflects a reliance on God's historical acts of salvation.  
  
3\. **[Scorn and Mockery](https://biblehub.com/topical/s/scorn_and_mockery.htm)**  
The psalmist describes being scorned and mocked by others, "All who see me mock me; they sneer and shake their heads" ([Psalm 22:7](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-7.htm)). This mirrors the mockery Jesus faced during His crucifixion.  
  
4\. **[Physical Suffering](https://biblehub.com/topical/p/physical_suffering.htm)**  
The psalmist vividly describes physical suffering, "I am poured out like water, and all my bones are disjointed" ([Psalm 22:14](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-14.htm)). This imagery is often associated with the physical agony of crucifixion.  
  
5\. **[Encirclement by Enemies](https://biblehub.com/topical/e/encirclement_by_enemies.htm)**  
The psalmist feels surrounded by enemies, "For dogs surround me; a band of evil men encircles me" ([Psalm 22:16](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-16.htm)). This can be seen as a metaphor for the hostile forces against Jesus.  
  
6\. **[Casting Lots for Garments](https://biblehub.com/topical/c/casting_lots_for_garments.htm)**  
The psalmist notes, "They divide my garments among them and cast lots for my clothing" ([Psalm 22:18](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-18.htm)). This specific event is directly fulfilled in the New Testament account of the crucifixion.  
  
7\. **[Prayer for Rescue](https://biblehub.com/topical/p/prayer_for_rescue.htm)**  
A plea for rescue and deliverance is made, "But You, O LORD, be not far off; O my Strength, come quickly to help me" ([Psalm 22:19](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-19.htm)). This reflects a deep yearning for divine intervention.  
  
8\. **[Declaration of Praise](https://biblehub.com/topical/d/declaration_of_praise.htm)**  
The tone shifts to praise and a vow to declare God's name, "I will proclaim Your name to my brothers; I will praise You in the assembly" ([Psalm 22:22](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-22.htm)). This signifies a transition from suffering to hope and future testimony.  
  
9\. **[Universal Worship](https://biblehub.com/topical/u/universal_worship.htm)**  
The psalm concludes with a vision of universal worship, "All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the LORD" ([Psalm 22:27](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-27.htm)). This anticipates the global impact of the Messiah's work.  
  
10\. **[Future Generations](https://biblehub.com/topical/f/future_generations.htm)**  
The psalmist speaks of future generations serving the Lord, "They will proclaim His righteousness to a people yet unborn" ([Psalm 22:31](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22-31.htm)). This points to the enduring legacy of God's salvation.

Topics

1\. **[Cry of Abandonment](https://biblehub.com/topical/c/cry_of_abandonment.htm)**  
The psalm begins with a profound sense of abandonment: "My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" ([Psalm 22:1](https://biblehub.com/psalms/22.htm)). This cry is echoed in the New Testament by Jesus on the cross, highlighting its messianic significance. The Hebrew word "עזבתני" (azavtani) conveys a deep sense of being left behind or forsaken.  
  
2\. **[Plea for Deliverance](https://biblehub.com/topical/p/plea_for_deliverance.htm)**
